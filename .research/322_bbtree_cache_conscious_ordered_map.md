## Verdict

**YES B+ corpus / lane-intel MEDIUM (storage lane, NOT the GPU league) / product-coverage YES — seeds a new family: ordered-structure internals.**

- **Corpus axis:** five KEEP-grade candidates, all CPU-side → route `rust_perf` (categories: caching, simd, dispatch, concurrency, allocation). The tempting WRONG domain is `kernel_opt` — nothing here is a GPU kernel; the GPU corpus's latency-hiding family (staged loaders, register double-buffer, look-ahead fusion) is the *analogue*, cited as kinship, not the home.
- **Lane-intel axis:** the llama.cpp league lanes (M3 prefill 0.53–0.57× FAIL, decode WON) are GPU-kernel lanes — bbtree moves none of them. The REAL lane mapping is the storage lane: `riir-neuron-db` Warm tier (`local_kv` scan family) and the restart story (`bbtree-mmap`: "first answer 160–2,500× sooner after restart, no rebuild" vs our WAL `resume_after` replay), plus the chain commitment-root ordered fold (below). Recorded as the manual-track issues, not league rows.
- **Product-coverage axis:** the corpus owns the *data-structure-choice* rule (`btreemap-sorted-iteration`) but NOTHING about how ordered structures themselves search — and every Rust service runs sorted-structure lookups (std BTreeMap internals, sorted Vec indexes, term dictionaries, route tables). This source seeds the family; the follow-up pool is the blog's own citation graph (all papers, listed above) + `microsoft/bf-tree` + arrow-rs #7748.
