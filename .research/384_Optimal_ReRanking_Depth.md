## TL;DR

**Distilled for the rulewright (modelless, inference-time):** re-ranking depth — how many candidates the expensive second stage sees — is a **per-query property**, not a constant. Deep re-ranking past a query's optimum stagnates or DEGRADES quality (the expensive stage promotes a worse tail candidate over a better head one). Standard QPP-family signals (score variance, stability, spread) FAIL to predict the per-query optimum; a discounted-gain read of ASSESSED first-stage quality (the paper's IR-DCG@10, computed with LLM labels) cuts mean re-ranking depth 3× at quality parity (oracle: 5× depth + 7% quality). The fusion for us: our **deterministic proposer-instantiation evidence** stands in for the paper's LLM judge — a DCG-discounted instantiation read over the retrieval pool should predict the per-span oracle pool depth where `pool_spread`/variance-family signals fail — and percentile depth calibration (family precedent already shipped in reflex `Posture::Percentile`) maps predictor → depth at a parity operating point. Consumers: the fixed `RERANK_POOL = 37` selection pool and the seat consult's whole-pool submission (live since plan 225 T5.2, `2623f4d09`).

## 1. Paper core findings

1. **Depth is query-specific.** Over 1000 MSMARCO DEV queries (BM25/SPLADE → MonoT5/MonoELECTRA), per-query depth-effectiveness trajectories split into monotone-improving, early-plateau, and clear-DEGRADING shapes (Fig. 2 — deeper re-ranking can finish below the first-stage list).
2. **`d*(q)` = shallowest depth attaining the query's max DCG@10** under dense LLM judgments; the oracle average optimal depth is ~3× shallower than full (BM25+MonoT5) and ~50× shallower for SPLADE (whose first stage is already strong — optimal avg depth ≈ 20; corroborates the fixed-≈20 RLT finding of Meng et al. 2024).
3. **QPP fails at depth prediction.** 11 standard predictors (NQC, SCNQC, QV-NQC, σmax, n(σx%), SMV, WIG, DenseQPP, UEF, RSD, OddsRatio) — the best (RSD) halves depth at parity; most need thr ≈ 0.96–1.00 (i.e. no saving).
4. **Assessed quality wins.** IR-DCG@10 — DCG@10 over the FIRST-STAGE ranking using LLM relevance labels — reaches parity at thr = 0.79 with mean depth 331 vs 1000 (3×), and ~15× on SPLADE. Oracle selection: +7% effectiveness at 5× depth reduction (BM25).
5. **Calibration recipe (closed-form):** equal-frequency bins of the predictor over K-fold train queries; per-bin depth = the p-th percentile of that bin's optimal depths; sweep p to the smallest operating point with `E[μ(d̂)/μ(full)] ≥ 1` (parity), then convert to a predictor threshold `thr_min`.
6. **Agentic framing:** the depth trajectory is the intervention signal — continue ranking vs route to reformulation/clarification. "Knowing when it does not know."

**Fusion (paper × headroom probe × jury signals × seat outcomes):** the paper pays an LLM judge for its assessed-quality labels; we get assessment **free and deterministic** — a proposer visitor that fires is a structural relevance label on a candidate rule. `inst_dcg(span) = Σ_{i≤k} fires(rule_i) / log2(i+1)` over the **first-stage (pre-rerank) similarity order**, reading only the top-k (k = 8 = `HEADROOM_DEPTHS[0]`), is the IR-DCG analog with zero inference-time judge cost. THE ORDER IS LOAD-BEARING: computed over the instantiation-RERANKED pool it collapses to a rescaled `instantiating` count (the rerank sorts `instantiates` first — `latent_matcher.rs` `all_rules` — so firing rules fill positions 1..m and the discounted sum is a monotone function of m, Spearman-identical to the undiscounted count; the positional discount carries information ONLY where the first-stage similarity order scatters firing rules across ranks). Probe accounting: similarity scores for the whole pool are already paid by retrieval; the predictor needs `fires()` for its top-k only (k walks), then instantiation-rerank probes max(k, d̂) candidates total vs today's 37 — the selection-pool arm saves probe walks, the hosted-bytes saving belongs to the seat arm (Phase 3) alone. Calibrate `inst_dcg → pool depth` percentile-wise from per-span oracle depths (fixtures now, real-file outcomes via fix_verify in Phase 0), then cap BOTH second stages per campaign: the selection pool (37 → d̂) and the seat submission (whole pool → d̂, `min_pool` floor kept). The paper's degradation finding (Fig. 2) predicts the seat cap is not merely cheaper — a leaner pool can also **improve** pick quality by removing tail candidates the seat would otherwise rank above better head ones.
